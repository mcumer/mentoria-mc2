# First-version project brief

Use this as a flexible output map, in the person's language. Use what is already
known; keep material gaps visible instead of filling every section with guesses.

1. **Direction and decision:** the idea, recommendation, person's choice and
   rationale. If not chosen yet, label the document as a proposal.
2. **User and situation:** who would use it, when, and the problem or job.
3. **Desired result:** observable improvement, baseline and success criterion
   if known. Attribute proposed measures and targets; leave unknowns explicit.
4. **Method to apply:** relevant steps, decision rules or exercises supplied
   by the person, including where human expertise remains necessary.
5. **Core experience:** inputs, the main interaction, expected output and the
   next action it enables. Avoid assuming AI is required for every function.
6. **First-version scope:** essential behavior and explicit exclusions. Put
   possible extensions in a short later list only when useful.
7. **Dependencies:** necessary content, data, tools, access and technical
   questions. Specify whether an integration is known or still to verify.
8. **Evidence and alternatives:** what supports the choice, important assumptions,
   simpler options and why they were chosen or set aside.
9. **Small test:** the assumption to test, practical prototype or manual trial,
   observations to collect and the result that would support or challenge the
   idea. Suggest a target only when invited; do not label a planned test as run.
10. **Next step:** the smallest practical action and open decisions. Include
    effort estimates only if justified and label uncertainty; do not invent
    a quote, deadline or supplier commitment.

For non-app or postponed decisions, adapt the same map to the useful next step.
Prefer a concise brief the person can review or share over an exhaustive spec.
